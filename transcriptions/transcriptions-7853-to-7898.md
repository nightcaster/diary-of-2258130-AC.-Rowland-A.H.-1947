**OCTOBER 3—Friday**

No mail in to-day.

Working. There is plenty to-do but there should be no trouble in the morning.

We are not getting payed until to-morrow due to the bank being closed.

I'm going to save as much of my wage as possible to-ward a watch.

**OCTOBER 4—Saturday**

No mail in.

Went into town to-day with Don, Doug & Jack & Dick – had a very good time.

Went to see Ben in B.G.H. – He is much better and hopes to be out next week. Went to the pictures to see "Song of the South". I haven't enjoyed a film so much for a long time. Had the usual feeds – it cost me more money than I intended to spend.

*Notes: "Ben" is my best reading of a slightly ambiguous name. I kept "payed" and the hyphenated "to-day", "to-morrow" and "to-ward" as written. The page numbers are 276 and 277.*

OCTOBER 13—Monday

Received a letter from Betty Slater dated 18th AUGUST!!!! It had been to Dum Dum and goodness knows where else.

I didn't get a wink of sleep last night. Those refs were up at about half three. Came back to the billet, went to bed in my clothes and slept like a log until half four.

Mauritius beat Drigh Rd at rugger this evening 11–0. Wizard show.

OCTOBER 14—Tuesday

Still no mail; it must be coming by sea.

Went into town with Hank and Don to watch a rugger match between Black Watch and the Brigade. The Brigade won and I'm glad too. The Black Watch played a dirty game.

On the way back we passed a shop that had some tables. Must call in on Friday.

- "refs" is kept as written, since I can't tell what it abbreviates.

- The last word is either "Friday" or "Tuesday". I read it as "Friday" from the opening stroke.

No mail in to-day.

We are going again on Sunday.

*Transcriber's notes: "Why" (21 Oct, line 2), "bunder-boating" and "4 Rs" are my best readings of the ink strokes. They are the least certain words on these pages. "Daks" and "Yorks" are left as written, and "wog" is kept verbatim as period language.*
